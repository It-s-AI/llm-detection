Detecting AI text, and learning to beat the detectors

Subnet 32 runs two competitions over the same question: can a machine tell AI-written text from human writing? Detection miners build the best detectors. Humanizer miners build models that rewrite AI text so that leading commercial detectors read it as human, while keeping the meaning and the quality.

How the humanizer competition works

One king, one challenger at a time, judged on hidden texts

Quality gates

How every rewrite is judged before the detectors see it

A humanizer could fool detectors by writing anything. The gates make sure it rewrites the same text: same facts, same language and genre, readable form. They are applied in order to every output, by LLM judges from two different vendors. A flag counts only when both judges raise it at the same place, taking the milder of their two ratings; when they disagree, a third model from another vendor acts as arbiter.

G0

Normalization

Unicode is normalized, invisible and control characters are removed, look-alike letters are mapped back. Everything is judged on the normalized text, and the output is cut at 1.3× the input's length.

GI

Injection screen

A dedicated judge looks for text addressed to the reviewers instead of the reader and must quote it. A flagged output is level 0 and goes no further. The output always reaches the judges as data inside a document tag, never as instructions.

G1

Meaning

When the period is built, the facts of every input are extracted and checked by both judges. Each rewrite is then checked both ways: every input fact must still be present and undistorted (recall), and every output sentence must be supported by the input (precision). Allowed: a few omissions, up to max(1, 10% of the facts), and a little filler, up to max(2, 20% of the sentences). Distortions and additions are not allowed.

G2

Language and genre

The rewrite must stay in the same language and the same genre, and read as a coherent whole. Register, format, person and tense may change freely: that is part of the humanizer's job.

G3

Naturalness

The judges list defects of form that the input did not have, each with a quote and a severity by its effect on the reader: minor, noticeable or gross. Only defects both judges found count. The allowance for minor ones grows with length: max(3, ⌈words / 75⌉).

The score

How a submission's score S is computed

Every period has about 500 hidden AI-written inputs and a reference set H of about 500 human texts written before 2022, matched by genre and length. The submission rewrites every input. Each rewrite gets a quality verdict from LLM judges and a score from each of the three detectors; the human texts are scored by the same detectors. S combines those numbers in four steps.

1

Quality level of each output

qi=mingateslevelg(i)∈{1,0.75,0.5,0}

Each rewrite first gets a quality grade from the gates described above: the lowest grade any gate gives it. The grade decides how much that rewrite can count for later. A perfect rewrite counts fully, a slightly flawed one counts partly, and a broken one counts as if every detector had caught it.

2

Two terms per detector

Ad=1−1n∑i=1nqiud,iPd=1n∑i=1nqi(1−sd,i)

For each detector we ask two questions. The first: can the detector still tell the rewrites apart from real human writing? We compare every rewrite with human texts of the same genre and length and count how often the detector finds the human text more suspicious. If it always picks out the rewrite, the detector wins completely; if it guesses no better than a coin, the rewrites are indistinguishable from people. The second: how sure is the detector? We take its average confidence that the rewrites were written by a human. Both answers are weighted by the quality grade, so broken text never helps.

3

Evasion per detector

Ed=12·2min(0.5,1−Ad)+12·PdEd∈[0,1]

The two answers are averaged into one number per detector, from 0 to 1. Half of it is earned by becoming truly indistinguishable from human writing. The other half is earned by any drop in the detector's confidence, so a miner sees progress even before the rewrites are good enough to pass as human. A 1 means the detector takes every rewrite for human writing.

4

One score across detectors

S=(13∑dEdp)1/p,p=−4

The three detector numbers are combined so that the weakest one dominates: the final score stays low until all three detectors are fooled, so beating two and ignoring the third does not pay. If any detector catches everything, the score is 0. The score runs from 0, which is what simply copying the AI text gets, to 1.

Incentives

Where the emission goes

Trust

Built to be checked, not trusted

Open code

Every line of the evaluator is public. Only the current period's texts and seeds are secret, and only until the period ends.

Committed on chain

Each period's inputs are fixed in advance by a commitment and a Merkle root posted on chain, so they cannot be changed after miners submit.

Verifiable afterwards

After a period ends, its texts, scores and crown decisions are published. Anyone can recompute every result with one script.

No human in the loop

Results come from commercial detectors, LLM judges and statistics. People only chose and calibrated the tools.

Statistical crown

A challenger must beat the king on the same texts with a paired bootstrap test, so luck does not take the crown.

One submission per registration

An identical copy only counts for whoever committed it on chain first, and the winning model is published 48 hours later.

Figures come from Subnet 32's own configuration and live checks on the production server (November 2026). Detector names are the commercial services the evaluator calls. The score is defined in the subnet's specification (D-20 to D-23).
