# 04b. G1 v2: meaning check — report

## Why it was redone
Diagnostics of G1 v1 (output = input, 20 pilot texts): **3 of 20 unchanged texts failed**. The error
per single check is ~2%, but the rule "minimum over 4 checks, ≤ 1 miss per ~13 facts" turned this into ~15%
rejections on a text that had not been changed at all. Misses: 11 — the checker saw a quote without context,
6 — quote selection, 3 — bad facts, 1 — technical. 15 of 18 false verdicts came from GPT-6 Luna; the worst cases were
fiction texts and dialogues.

## How G1 v2 works (`gates/meaning.py`)
1. **Facts — at period assembly** (`prepare_facts`). A model writes out atomic facts literally, with
   references to input sentence numbers. Each fact is then checked by **both judge families on the input itself**;
   a fact is kept only if both said "present". Bad facts are filtered out before the competition, not on miners'
   outputs.
2. **Deterministic pass.** A fact whose input sentences appear unchanged in the output is credited without an LLM.
   So is an output sentence that appears verbatim in the input. Comparison ignores case, whitespace and
   typography; sentences shorter than 4 words count only on an exact match. An unchanged text passes without
   a single judge call.
3. **Completeness (recall).** The judge sees the whole output and, for each fact, the source sentence (context: who
   is speaking, about what). Verdict: "present" / "omitted" / "distorted" (a different number, a negation, who did what to whom
   mixed up, a different cause or attribution).
4. **Precision.** For each changed output sentence, checked against the whole input: "present" /
   "embellishment" (no new verifiable information) / "added" (new facts, numbers, names, studies,
   examples) / "distorted". Facts are no longer re-extracted from the output.
5. **Aggregation.** Two judges from different vendors answer on each open item; where their classes diverge,
   the arbiter (a strong model) decides. "Present" and "embellishment" are one class and do not count as a dispute.
6. **Thresholds are in counts** (`G1Config`): omissions ≤ max(1, 10% of facts); distortions 0; additions 0;
   "filler" sentences (both judges: "embellishment") ≤ max(2, 20% of **input** sentences — relative to the input, so that
   padding does not raise its own limit).
7. **Judge error** (no response, broken JSON, no verdict for an item) → status `error` (rerun), never
   a rejection for the miner.
8. **Response cache** (`llm/cache.py`): rechecking the same output is free; the values are encrypted
   with the period key (they contain fragments of hidden texts).

Injection protection is the same: the text under check is only inside `<document>`, our `[n]` markers inside
miner sentences are neutralized (`[3]` → `(3)`), and responses follow a JSON schema.

## Calibration (`scripts/calibrate_g1.py`)
On a stratified sample of pilot inputs (3 per genre), variants with a known answer are built:

| Should pass | Should fail | Unlabeled (reviewed by humans) |
|---|---|---|
| identity, typography, light_edit (synonyms, all sentences in place) | drop (sentences removed), truncate (tail cut off), contradict (one sentence made false), insert (one new specific claim), pad (5 sentences without content) | paraphrase (strong-LLM paraphrase), phrasly (commercial humanizer) |

The variants are generated by a model that is not among the judge candidates (Grok 4.7). Each candidate labels each
open item once; the arbiters label the union of the disputed items of all pairs. Then all "pair +
arbiter" configurations are computed offline with the same code as in production (`run_two_family` + `decide_g1`).

### Results (24 inputs × 10 variants, 5 candidates, 2 arbiters; $4.87)
Share of correct decisions (for "unlabeled" — share that passed):

| Configuration (judges \| arbiter) | identity / typography / light_edit | drop | truncate | contradict | insert | pad | paraphrase | phrasly | $ / output |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **GPT-6 Luna + Gemini 3.8 Flash \| Claude Sonnet 5.5** (selected) | **1.00 / 1.00 / 1.00** | 0.96 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.88 | 1.00 | 0.25 | 0.010 |
| Claude Haiku 5.5 + Gemini 3.8 Flash \| Sonnet 5.5 | 1.00 / 1.00 / 1.00 | 0.92 | 0.96 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.88 | 1.00 | 0.38 | 0.009 |
| Haiku 5.5 + GPT-6 Luna \| GPT-6.1 Sol | 1.00 / 1.00 / 1.00 | 0.92 | 0.88 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.88 | 0.96 | 0.17 | 0.005 |
| Gemini 3.8 Flash + GPT-6.1 Terra \| Sonnet 5.5 | 1.00 / 1.00 / 1.00 | 0.96 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.75 | 1.00 | 0.21 | 0.015 |
| Haiku 5.5 + GPT-6 Luna, **no arbiter** | 1.00 / 1.00 / 1.00 (5 errors) | 0.71 (17 errors) | 1.00 (16 errors) | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.90 | 1.00 (5 errors) | — (24 errors) | 0.002 |

Conclusions:
- **No configuration with an arbiter has false rejections on correct edits** (0 of 72: unchanged text,
  typography, synonyms); a strong-LLM paraphrase passes 24/24 with the selected one. In v1 it was 3 of 20 on unchanged text.
- **Distortions and inserted facts are always caught** (24/24 and 24/24).
- Omissions: 1 of 24 "drop" passed — the deleted sentence of a forum post is paraphrased elsewhere, so the judges
  are right. "pad" is not caught on 3 long texts (25–30 sentences): 5 "filler" sentences there are less than 20% —
  that is how the threshold is set.
- **An arbiter is required**: the pair's disputes remain unresolved (error → rerun), and on the humanizer this happens for all 24.
  The arbiter is rarely needed on normal edits (0.4–0.6% of items) and often on questionable ones (15% for Phrasly).
- GPT-6 Luna, the worst in v1 (15 of 18 false verdicts), works well in v2: the problem was the check
  format (a quote without context), not the model.
- Facts: 439 extracted, 437 passed the check by both families; 13 of 364 sentences (≥ 4 words) were left without facts —
  their deletion is invisible to recall, but visible to precision if they were replaced with something new.

### Cost
G1 per output: ~$0.010 (less for good outputs — some sentences pass without an LLM, and the arbiter is almost never
needed). Facts at period assembly: extraction $0.0005 + check by a judge pair ~$0.006 per input (in calibration
the check was done by Sonnet 5.5 + GPT-6.1 Sol, $0.024 per input: it filtered out 2 facts of 439 — for production a judge pair
is enough). Per submission with n = 750: G1 ≈ $7.5, facts per period ≈ $5.

## Phrasly on all 78 pilot outputs (all gates)
`scripts/pilot_phrasly.py` → `scripts/review_phrasly.py` (judges from `configs/gates.json`).

| Gate | Passed |
|---|---|
| G1 meaning | **18 / 78** (23%) |
| G2 style | 31 / 78 |
| G3 naturalness | 1 / 78 |
| all three | 0 / 78 |

- G1 rejection reasons (a text can have several): additions 50, distortions 44, omissions 8, "filler" 1.
  Checked manually on examples: the judges are right — Phrasly changes quotes ("physiological disconnect" →
  "biomechanical problem"), attribution (committee → "the league's director of physicians"), invents details (NFL, "randomization",
  BMI/HbA1c), reverses claims ("letters strip away polish" → "letters achieve polish").
- G1 by genre: articles/blogs 7/12, letters 4/8, essays 4/23, forums 2/8, scientific 1/7, news 0/8, reviews 0/8,
  fiction 0/4.
- Borderline cases are narrowing without distortion ("social media ads" → "Facebook ads"): with zero tolerance for
  additions this is a rejection. Decision — after review by the owner.
- **G2/G3 are not calibrated.** G2 most often flags "register" and "person/tense" (Phrasly makes the text more colloquial).
  G3: almost all "introduced errors / odd words" flags are set by GPT-6 Luna, and Gemini sets none; the v1 rule "any
  judge's flag = rejection" produces the same skew here that G1 v1 had. Next step — the same scheme for G2/G3:
  two judges + an arbiter for disputes, calibration variants (inserted typos, cut-offs, register/person changes).
- Review page (12 cases, 6 passed / 6 did not): the "Phrasly on G1" artifact.
